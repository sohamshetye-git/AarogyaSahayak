# ICMR Clinical Management Protocols - Acute Coronary Syndrome & Chest Pain in Primary Care
Issuing Authority: Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR)
Document ID: DOC-ICMR-CVD-003
Publication Date: 2023-08-20
Source: https://icmr.gov.in/guidelines/stw_acute_chest_pain.pdf

## Section 1: Clinical Presentation and Red Flags
Acute chest pain represents a potential medical emergency requiring rapid triage to distinguish life-threatening conditions (Acute Coronary Syndrome, Aortic Dissection, Pulmonary Embolism) from non-cardiac causes.
High-Risk Red Flags include:
- Retrosternal constricting, crushing, or heavy chest discomfort lasting >15 minutes.
- Radiation of pain to left arm, neck, jaw, or epigastrium.
- Associated diaphoresis (profuse cold sweating), nausea, vomiting, dizziness, or syncope.
- Shortness of breath (dyspnea) at rest or on minimal exertion.
- Vital sign abnormalities: Systolic BP < 90 mmHg (cardiogenic shock) or > 180 mmHg (hypertensive crisis), irregular pulse, or SpO2 < 94%.

## Section 2: PHC Level Immediate Management Protocol
1. Immediate Rest: Place patient in comfortable semi-fowler position. Administer supplemental oxygen if SpO2 < 94%.
2. 12-Lead ECG Acquisition: Obtain and interpret 12-lead ECG within 10 minutes of arrival.
3. Loading Dose Pharmacotherapy: Unless contraindicated (active GI bleed, severe allergy), administer Aspirin 300 mg chewable + Clopidogrel 300 mg loading dose orally. If systolic BP > 100 mmHg, administer sublingual Nitroglycerin 0.5 mg.
4. Urgent Referral: Arrange urgent emergency transport (108 Ambulance) to nearest Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI) capable center or district hospital for reperfusion therapy.
