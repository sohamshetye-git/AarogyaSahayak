# ICMR Standard Treatment Workflow - Hypertensive Disorders in Pregnancy
Issuing Authority: Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR)
Document ID: DOC-ICMR-MCH-001
Publication Date: 2024-02-15
Source: https://icmr.gov.in/guidelines/stw_hypertension_pregnancy.pdf

## Section 1: Definition and Classification
Hypertensive disorders of pregnancy (HDP) complicate approximately 5-10% of all pregnancies in India and represent a leading cause of maternal and perinatal morbidity and mortality. 
1. Gestational Hypertension: Systolic Blood Pressure >= 140 mmHg and/or Diastolic Blood Pressure >= 90 mmHg arising de novo after 20 weeks of gestation in the absence of significant proteinuria.
2. Pre-eclampsia: New-onset hypertension (BP >= 140/90 mmHg) occurring after 20 weeks gestation accompanied by proteinuria (>= 1+ on dipstick or >= 300 mg/24h) or maternal organ dysfunction including thrombocytopenia, renal insufficiency, impaired liver function, pulmonary edema, or persistent cerebral/visual disturbances.
3. Severe Pre-eclampsia: Systolic BP >= 160 mmHg and/or Diastolic BP >= 110 mmHg recorded on two occasions at least 4 hours apart while the patient is on bed rest, or systolic BP >= 140/90 mmHg accompanied by headache, scotomata, epigastric pain, or hyperreflexia.

## Section 2: Clinical Warning Signs (Red Flags)
Any pregnant woman beyond 20 weeks of gestation presenting with the following danger signs requires immediate evaluation and referral:
- Severe frontal or occipital headache unresponsive to standard analgesia.
- Visual disturbances including blurring of vision, flashing lights (scotomata), or temporary loss of vision.
- Persistent right upper quadrant or epigastric pain indicating hepatic capsular distension.
- Rapidly progressive bilateral pedal edema, facial swelling, or marked sudden weight gain (>1 kg/week).
- Hyperreflexia and clonus (>3 beats) indicating central nervous system hyperexcitability.

## Section 3: Primary Healthcare (PHC) Level Management
At the Primary Health Center level:
1. Triage & Stabilization: Place the patient in left lateral tilt to optimize uteroplacental perfusion.
2. Repeat Blood Pressure Measurement: Verify with calibrated mercury or validated digital sphygmomanometer using appropriate cuff size after 15 minutes of rest.
3. Urine Albumin Screening: Immediate point-of-care dipstick testing for albuminuria.
4. Pharmacotherapy Initiation: For confirmed sustained systolic BP >= 150 mmHg or diastolic BP >= 100 mmHg, oral antihypertensive therapy must be initiated. Labetalol 100 mg orally twice daily is the first-line drug of choice in pregnancy. Alternatively, oral Methyldopa (250 mg thrice daily) or Nifedipine sustained release (20 mg daily) may be utilized. ACE inhibitors, ARBs, and Atenolol are strictly contraindicated in pregnancy due to fetotoxicity.
5. Referral Coordination: Promptly refer to First Referral Unit (FRU) or District Hospital with emergency obstetric transport if severe features or fetal distress are identified.
