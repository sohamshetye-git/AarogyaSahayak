# ICMR Integrated Management of Neonatal and Childhood Illness (IMNCI) - Pediatric Acute Fever & Dehydration
Issuing Authority: Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR)
Document ID: DOC-ICMR-PED-005
Publication Date: 2023-09-12
Source: https://icmr.gov.in/guidelines/imnci_pediatric_fever.pdf

## Section 1: Evaluation of Pediatric Acute Fever
Fever in young children (<5 years) requires rigorous assessment for general danger signs.
General Danger Signs in Under-5s:
- Inability to drink, breastfeed, or take fluids.
- Vomiting everything ingested.
- Convulsions during current illness episode.
- Lethargy, floppiness, or unconsciousness.
- High grade fever > 38.5 C (101.3 F) lasting >3 days or associated with stiff neck/petechial rash.

## Section 2: Assessment of Dehydration Severity
- No Dehydration: Alert, normal eyes, drinks eagerly, skin pinch goes back quickly. Manage at home with Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS) Plan A.
- Some Dehydration: Restless, irritable, sunken eyes, drinks eagerly/thirsty, skin pinch goes back slowly (<2 seconds). Manage at PHC with ORS Plan B (75 ml/kg over 4 hours) + Zinc supplementation (20 mg daily for 14 days; 10 mg if <6 months).
- Severe Dehydration: Lethargic or floppy, sunken eyes, unable to drink, skin pinch goes back very slowly (>2 seconds). Immediate Plan C: IV Ringers Lactate (100 ml/kg) and urgent hospital transfer.

## Section 3: Antipyretic Guidance and Safety Rules
- Paracetamol (10-15 mg/kg per dose every 4-6 hours, maximum 60 mg/kg/day) is the only approved antipyretic for primary pediatric use.
- Aspirin and adult NSAIDs are strictly contraindicated in children due to risk of Reye syndrome and acute kidney injury.
