# MoHFW ASHA Field Manual - Maternal Danger Signs & High Risk Pregnancy Triage
Issuing Authority: Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoHFW)
Document ID: DOC-MOHFW-ASHA-002
Publication Date: 2023-11-10
Source: https://nhm.gov.in/guidelines/asha_maternal_danger_signs.pdf

## Section 1: Role of ASHA Worker in Antenatal Surveillance
The Accredited Social Health Activist (ASHA) is the frontline community health link for early identification and home tracking of high-risk pregnant women.
1. Identification: Every pregnant woman must receive at least 4 essential antenatal check-ups (ANC), tetanus toxoid immunization, and daily Iron Folic Acid (IFA) supplementation.
2. Home Visit Tracking: For women identified with high-risk pregnancy (HRP) factors, ASHA must conduct structured home visits at least every 3 to 7 days.

## Section 2: Recognition of Maternal Red Flag Danger Signs
During home visits, ASHA must screen for and recognize the following maternal danger signs:
- Severe and continuous headache during 2nd or 3rd trimester.
- Blurring of vision or spots before eyes.
- Sudden swelling (edema) of hands, face, or feet that does not subside after overnight rest.
- Vaginal bleeding or watery fluid discharge (rupture of membranes).
- Decreased or absent fetal movements.
- High fever with chills or persistent vomiting.
- Blood pressure measurement showing Systolic >= 140 mmHg or Diastolic >= 90 mmHg.

## Section 3: Field Action and Emergency Referral Protocol
When any danger sign or elevated blood pressure is detected:
1. Immediate Reassurance & Contact: Calm the mother and family. Explain the urgency without causing panic.
2. Direct Escalation: Immediately inform the Auxiliary Nurse Midwife (ANM) and Medical Officer at the nearest Primary Health Center (PHC).
3. Referral Arrangement: Organize 102/108 emergency ambulance transport. Accompany the pregnant mother to the PHC or Sub-District Hospital with her Mother and Child Protection (MCP) card.
4. Follow-up Adherence: Post-consultation, verify that prescribed antihypertensives (such as Labetalol) and calcium supplements are being taken as directed by the doctor.
